Inter-American Institute for Cooperation on Agriculture

Message from the Director General

“The Americas has a historic opportunity to consolidate its role as the key regional supplier of food and nutrition for a global population of almost 10 billion people, providing healthy diets based on more efficient and sustainable production systems. The region has unique advantages—natural capital, science, production diversity, and institutional capacity. However, harnessing these assets to assume a leadership role calls for evidence-based decision-making, strategic investment, solid partnerships, and responsive technical cooperation, geared towards achieving concrete results for countries and rural territories.”

What is IICA?

The Inter-American Institute for Cooperation on Agriculture (IICA) is the specialized agency for agriculture and rural development, with a mission to encourage, promote, and support the efforts of its thirty-four Member States in the Americas to achieve agricultural development and rural well-being through technical cooperation of excellence. Its proposed course of action is summarized in Resolution 545 of the Inter-American Board of Agriculture (IABA), adopted in 2023, in which the Institute expands its technical cooperation portfolio to include seven technical programs. IICA’s strategy is outlined in its 2022-2026 Medium-term Plan, a business model with objectives that set out how the Institute will add value to its member countries, and five strategic statements that provide the framework for the expected results of its technical cooperation.

New generation of public policies

The Institute is assisting the countries of the Americas to transition to a new generation of public policies for agrifood systems, based on evidence, technical dialogue, and regional cooperation. To that end, it is fostering the generation of strategic information; facilitating opportunities for collaboration among governments, international organizations, research centers, and production stakeholders; and providing support for the development of mechanisms that will facilitate better decision-making, more targeted investments, and the scaling up of effective solutions.

Technical cooperation of excellence

The technical cooperation of the Institute in 2025 sought to transform the priorities of the Member States into concrete solutions, stronger institutional capacities, and verifiable results for agriculture and rural life in the Americas. IICA’s hemispheric presence enabled it to serve as a technical catalyst, a partnership facilitator, and a bridge between evidence, innovation, public policies, markets, and territories. The results achieved reflect cooperation with a focus on generating practical value for the countries, by modernizing institutions, strengthening capacities, mobilizing resources, facilitating trade, promoting innovation, and providing support in building more sustainable, competitive, and resilient production processes.

Low-methane rice program

Together with Chile’s NARI and the Global Methane Hub, low-methane rice cultivation was promoted in the Southern Cone, through the implementation of efficient irrigation systems in Maule and Nuble (Chile).

This model achieved a drastic reduction in water use (from 25,000 to 11,000 m³ per hectare) and reduced methane emissions.

Efforts were undertaken in Chile to promote this system in Argentina, Brazil, Ecuador, and Uruguay.

In collaboration with the Climate & Clean Air Coalition (CCAC), the Institute developed a roadmap to provide producers with access to carbon markets and payment for environmental services systems.

This system consolidated its role as a key platform in the administration and provision of IICA’s library services, bymanaging 152,552 information resources and documents, including bibliographic materials, technical documents, and specialized collections to support technical cooperation, research, and training. The platform facilitates integrated management of library processes, including cataloging, circulation, collection control, and user services, while also providing technological support to the Orton Memorial Library (IICA/CATIE).
